# Lean formalization: cotransverse cube maps in dimensions three and four

This Lean project formalizes a collection of related arguments about
cotransverse maps of cubes:

1. the proposed three-dimensional counterexample in the proof of Theorem
   3.1.16 of *Combinatorics of labelling in higher dimensional automata* is
   generated after all;
2. every cotransverse endomorphism in dimensions at most three is generated
   by the expected level generators, whereas an explicit counterexample
   exists in dimension four;
3. the same four-dimensional map does not belong to the closure generated by
   maps of $\widehat{\square}$ and coordinate permutations.

The terminology follows the more recent paper *Towards a theory of natural
directed paths*: $\widehat{\square}_S$ denotes the category of all
cotransverse maps, and $\widehat{\square}$ denotes the maximal thick
category of cubes not containing the permutations.

The sources target:

- Lean **4.33.1**;
- mathlib **v4.33.1**.

## Main results

### The cube model

The formalization represents the vertices of the $n$-cube by finite subsets
of $\mathrm{Fin}(n)$:

| Mathematics | Lean |
| --- | --- |
| vertex of the $n$-cube | `Cube n := Finset (Fin n)` |
| directed edge $A\to B$ | `DirectedEdge A B`, meaning $B=A\cup\{i\}$ for some $i\notin A$ |
| cotransverse map | `Cotransverse f`, meaning that `f` preserves directed edges |
| coordinate permutation | `permMap σ` |
| elementary two-coordinate fold | `comparator p q` |
| word in permutations and folds | `Generated f` |

Lean uses zero-based coordinates. Thus the path $P_4$ appearing in the
four-dimensional construction has vertices $0,1,2,3$ and edges
$\{0,1\},\{1,2\},\{2,3\}$.

### Dimension three

The first file proves:

- `oldMap_factorization`: the alleged counterexample is $c_{2,3}\circ c_{1,3}$.

  Consequently, `oldMap_generated` proves that it is generated and hence is
  not a counterexample.
- `dimension_zero`, `dimension_one`, `dimension_two`, and
  `dimension_three`: in each of these dimensions, cotransversality is
  equivalent to membership in the generated level monoid.
- `cotransverse_normal_form_three`: every cotransverse endomorphism of the
  three-cube has one of the five normal-form types obtained from the
  bipartite incidence cycle $C_6$.
- `cotransverseMaps3_card`: there are exactly **66** cotransverse
  endomorphisms of the three-cube.

The theorem `source_three_of_coface_factorization` records the corresponding
heterodimensional deduction. The coface-factorization theorem cited in the
mathematical paper is not postulated as a Lean axiom: its existence and
cotransversality clauses are explicit hypotheses of the theorem.

### The dimension-four witness

The explicit map `CotransverseDimensionFour.F` is determined by rank and by
the path graph $P_4$:

$$
F(A)=
\begin{cases}
\varnothing, & |A|=0,\\
\{0\}, & |A|=1,\\
\{0,1\}, & |A|=2\text{ and }A\text{ is an edge of }P_4,\\
\{0,2\}, & |A|=2\text{ and }A\text{ is not an edge of }P_4,\\
\{0,1,2\}, & |A|=3,\\
\{0,1,2,3\}, & |A|=4.
\end{cases}
$$

The key theorem chain is:

- `F_cotransverse`: $F$ preserves every directed edge;
- `path4_no_twins`: distinct vertices of $P_4$ have distinct open
  neighbourhoods;
- `F_no_global_fold`: $F$ globally folds no pair of coordinates;
- `F_not_injective`: $F$ is not injective;
- `F_not_generated`: a noninjective generated word must have a first fold,
  contradicting `F_no_global_fold`;
- `strict_in_dimension_four`: there is a cotransverse endomorphism of the
  four-cube which is not generated by permutations and comparators.

### Adding maps of $\widehat{\square}$ and permutations

The second file proves a stronger non-generation statement. The structure
`Transverse4 h` deliberately describes a class at least as large as the
actual endomorphisms of $\widehat{\square}$. It records only the following
three consequences of Proposition 2.13 of *Towards a theory of natural
directed paths*:

1. `h` is cotransverse;
2. if its action on rank-one vertices is injective, then `h` is the identity;
3. its colours satisfy the monotonicity forced by injective ordered
   two-face restrictions.

The important results are:

- `source_dimension_le_target` and
  `intermediate_dimension_eq_four`: every intermediate cube in a
  cotransverse factorization from the four-cube back to itself also has
  dimension four;
- `path4_prime`: the kernel/module argument for $P_4$;
- `six_colour_obstruction_four`: the exact four-vertex edge-colouring
  obstruction;
- `F_not_symGenerated`: $F$ is absent even from the enlarged closure
  generated by `Transverse4` maps and coordinate permutations;
- `cotransverse_not_generated_by_transverse_and_symmetries`: the requested
  existential result;
- `F_not_generated_by_actual_and_symmetries`: any actual class `T`
  contained in `Transverse4` also has symmetric closure not containing
  $F$.

The final wrapper keeps the bridge to the paper explicit:

    hincluded : ∀ h, T h → Transverse4 h

Proposition 2.13 supplies this inclusion when `T` is the class of
four-dimensional endomorphisms of $\widehat{\square}$. The Lean project
does not silently assume that proposition as an axiom.

## Trust and formalization boundary

- The files contain no `sorry`, `admit`, custom `axiom`, or
  `native_decide`.
- Finite statements are sometimes proved with `decide`. This produces a
  proof term checked by Lean's kernel; it is not an untrusted external
  certificate.
- The dimension-three normal-form certificate checks the $3^3\cdot3^3=729$
  possible pairs of maps on the two parts of the incidence graph.
- In the audited build, the principal results reported only Lean's standard
  logical dependencies `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`.
- The project formalizes the stated cube-map arguments, not all of either
  source paper. In particular, the cited coface factorization and the bridge
  from Proposition 2.13 are exposed as hypotheses at the points where they
  are used.
